# Doctor of Communication

## Program Description

The Doctor of Communication (DCOMM) program is designed to strengthen the capability of practitioners by bringing the academic rigors of research, critical analysis, intellectual advancement, and science skills into the profession of communication as practiced in knowledge industries such as the academe, R&D institutions, government, and development-assistance agencies.

The program offers a comprehensive curriculum that combines theoretical exploration, rigorous research, and practical application. A distinguishing feature is its emphasis on original research — students explore cutting-edge topics, critically analyze existing theories, and generate new knowledge in the field of communication.

The program also emphasizes practical application, encouraging students to bridge theory and practice through experiential learning opportunities such as internships, collaborations with industry partners, or fieldwork. Upon completion, graduates are prepared to assume leadership roles in academia, research institutions, government agencies, non-profit organizations, consulting firms, and media organizations.

## Program Goals

The DCOMM program prepares graduates to:

- Apply advanced communication theories and research skills to address contemporary communication challenges;
- Conduct original, independent research that contributes to the advancement of the communication discipline;
- Assume leadership roles in communication practice across the academe, R&D, government, and development sectors;
- Apply expertise in communication policy, organizational communication, knowledge management, and information technologies; and
- Make a positive impact on society through critical analysis and innovative communication solutions.

## Mode of Instruction

Online (Distance Education). All DComm courses are delivered online (asynchronous).

## Curriculum

The DComm program consists of: 4 Core Courses (12 units), 7 Specialized Courses (21 units), and a Doctoral Dissertation (12 units). **Total: 45 units.**

## Courses

### Core Courses (12 units)

| Course Code | Course Title | Description | Prerequisite |
|-------------|--------------|-------------|--------------|
| COMM 310 | Communication Theory and Practice | Contemporary communication theories and practices; communication models; approaches to theorizing in communication | None |
| COMM 390 | Communication Research Paradigms | Foundations of communication research; basic assumptions, concepts and approaches to communication research | COMM 310 |
| COMM 391 | Communication Research Methodologies | Quantitative and qualitative approaches to communication research | COMM 390 |
| COMM 399 | Online Colloquium in Communication Research | Student-managed online colloquium focused on selected topics related to the systematic study of communication | COMM 391 |

### Specialized Courses (21 units)

| Course Code | Course Title | Description |
|-------------|--------------|-------------|
| COMM 320 | Management of Communication Media Resources & Systems | Concepts and structures of communication systems; application of contemporary management theories in effective operations of communication systems |
| COMM 330 | Communication Policy and Planning | Current approaches to communication policy sciences and planning at organizational, national, and global levels |
| COMM 340 | Organizational Communication | Concepts, techniques, and contemporary thinking in organizing and managing corporate communication systems |
| COMM 350 | Information and Knowledge Management | Theoretical foundations, principles, and development applications of Information and Knowledge Management |
| COMM 360 | Information & Communication Media Technologies | Current and emerging ICTs and their application to the generation, processing, storage, and utilization of information |
| COMM 370 | Corporate Social Responsibility Communication | Philosophical, social, and moral foundations of corporate social responsibility in a developing society |
| COMM 380 | Advocacy and Social Mobilization | Contemporary principles in and approaches to advocacy and social mobilization in a changing social environment |

### Dissertation

| Course Code | Course Title | Description | Units |
|-------------|--------------|-------------|-------|
| COMM 400 | Doctoral Dissertation | Comprehensive and exhaustive directed independent study consisting of in-depth analysis of communication phenomena or synthesis of scientific communication literature | 12 |

*Students may only enroll in COMM 400 after completing all coursework. The final grade is issued once the dissertation has been completed and defended.*
