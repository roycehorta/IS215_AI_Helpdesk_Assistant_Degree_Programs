# Master of International Health

## Program Description

International health is a field focusing on public health across regional or national boundaries. It seeks to understand health problems in a global context; compare factors that affect the health of people in resource-poor and transitional economies around the world; and develop means of disease reduction and health protection and promotion for these populations.

The program is designed for medical and other professionals working in health and development (nursing, dentistry, veterinary medicine, allied medical professions, social work); entering and mid-career health professionals seeking graduate-level education for supervisory roles; those pursuing an academic career in international health; and students with international and cross-cultural interests.

*Note: Admission to the MIH is through the Diploma in International Health (DIH) program. A student who has completed the 24-unit DIH requirement may opt to proceed to the Master's program by completing an additional 12 units upon compliance with MIH admission requirements and passing the Qualifying Exam.*

## Program Goals

The program aims to develop international health professionals who are able to:

- Design health programs that address significant global health problems informed by a critical understanding of global political, economic, environmental, and cultural systems;
- Implement services that work in a global context for health promotion and development in accordance with international and local policies, directions, and ethical standards;
- Evaluate health development programs and strategies across different settings, both local and international, especially in middle- and low-resource countries;
- Engage in effective collaboration with important international organizations and NGOs for the improvement of health; and
- Contribute to the continuously evolving discipline of international health through research generation.

## Mode of Instruction

Online (Distance Education), semestral basis

## Curriculum

The Master's program consists of 6 units of thesis and 30 units of course work: four core courses (12 units), four major courses (12 units) in more focused health areas, and two cognate courses (6 units). *(24 units of course work are earned from the Diploma program.)*
