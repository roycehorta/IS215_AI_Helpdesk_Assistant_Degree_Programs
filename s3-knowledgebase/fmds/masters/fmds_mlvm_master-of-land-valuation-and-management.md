# Master of Land Valuation and Management

## Program Description

The Master of Land Valuation and Management (MLVM) program is designed to address the need for high quality valuation education that will be accessible to professional land valuers and property managers, most of who are working in banks, private appraisal companies and public assessor's offices in regional, provincial and municipal governments. The program also caters to those with no background in land valuation and management who wish to enter the profession.

The MLVM and the Diploma in Land Valuation and Management (DLVM) are ladderized programs. Graduates of the DLVM may continue on to the MLVM program, which requires an additional year to complete a thesis and two additional courses.

## Program Goals

The program aims to:

- Develop professional valuers and property managers whose standards of practice and service shall be globally competitive and will promote the growth of the real estate industry;
- Develop professional valuers and property managers who thoroughly understand the land valuation and management sector's potential and actual contribution to sustainable development; and
- Contribute to the land valuation and management body of knowledge by encouraging research in this field.

## Mode of Instruction

Courses are delivered on a semestral basis using various technologies where appropriate. Virtual classrooms run in MyPortal, the learning management system of UPOU.

## Curriculum

The MLVM is a ladderized program. Students must first complete the DLVM program (8 courses, 24 units) before applying for the MLVM. The MLVM consists of 2 additional courses (6 units) plus a 6-unit thesis. **Total MLVM units: 12 (additional to DLVM's 24 units).**

## Courses

| Course Code | Course Title | Description | Units |
|-------------|--------------|-------------|-------|
| LVM 201 | Concepts and Processes of Land Valuation | Concepts, nature, practice and process of land valuation | 3 |
| LVM 202 | Development Controls and Construction | Elements of the built environment that influence property values | 3 |
| LVM 203 | Land Laws and Planning for Development | Contemporary land and real property laws, public administration and institutional systems related to land use planning and regulation | 3 |
| LVM 204 | Statutory Valuation | Elements of government valuation including property taxation, assessment, and code of ethics | 3 |
| LVM 205 | Advanced Land Valuation | Principles of valuation of complex properties and concepts of property value, market analysis, and investment properties | 3 |
| LVM 206 | Land Asset Management | Functions, roles and responsibilities in property asset management within physical, economic, legal and financial constraints | 3 |
| LVM 207 | Agricultural Valuation | Valuation of agricultural properties with emphasis on their productivity | 3 |
| LVM 208 | Mass Appraisal | Valuation of multiple properties including statistical analysis and computer-assisted mass appraisal systems | 3 |
